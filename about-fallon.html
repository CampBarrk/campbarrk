Meet Fallon Barr

The medical mind behind Camp Barrk

Fallon Barr is the founder of Camp Barrk, a dog daycare and boarding service for active dogs in Brooklyn, Queens and Manhattan. She brings 15 years of veterinary experience to the care of every dog in her program, as a Former Licensed Veterinary Technician and a Fear Free certified practitioner in a veterinary setting. She started Camp Barrk to give city dogs the exercise they need without the injuries that come from too much, too fast.

Her background

Fallon has spent her career across animal hospitality and veterinary medicine:

  • Veterinary experience: 15 years. Former Licensed Veterinary Technician.
  • Wildlife rehabilitation: started as a volunteer at The Wild Bird Fund in Manhattan.
  • Clinical medicine: Bay Ridge Animal Hospital, then Marine Park Veterinary Group as their primary surgical technician with a focus in anesthesiology.
  • Dog walking & care: dog walker at Bark Slope and manager for Brooklyn Bark.
  • Before Camp Barrk: Fallon ran Hotel Barrk, where her approach to care first built a loyal client base.
  • Certifications: Former Licensed Veterinary Technician; Fear Free certified in a veterinary setting.

Why a veterinary background matters in daycare

Most daycares measure a good day by how tired a dog is. Fallon measures it by how that dog is doing a year from now.

She builds stamina slowly.

New dogs start with shorter, easier outings. Distance and intensity increase step by step, so muscles and joints get stronger before they're asked to do more.

She knows the breed risks.

Some breeds are prone to luxating patellas. Others are prone to elbow problems or torn ACLs. Fallon plans each dog's activity around those risks instead of finding out the hard way.

She adjusts as dogs age.

An active dog at two and the same dog at nine need different work. Fallon keeps aging dogs moving to protect strength and joint health, without tipping them over the edge.

She is comfortable with medical needs.

Many Camp Barrk dogs have irritable bowel and other digestive issues. Owners trust Fallon to follow feeding plans exactly, spot early warning signs and tell them when something needs a vet's attention.

Her philosophy: the dog comes first

Fallon will tell you if Camp Barrk isn't right for your dog. Some dogs need a quieter program, a slower start or a vet visit first. Saying so is part of the job.

A trusted resource for owners

Clients often come to Fallon with questions between vet visits: Is this limp worth a call? Is this diet working? Is my dog ready for longer hikes? She is a knowledgeable second set of eyes who helps owners know when to call their vet. She does not diagnose or replace veterinary care.

Camp Barrk is not a veterinary practice and does not replace your vet. We work alongside your vet's guidance.
